RELEASE NOTES

RehabAI’s first release

By RehabAI · September 20, 2026

September 20 marked the first release of RehabAI and an important step toward making guided at-home exercise more interactive and measurable.

What was included

The first release brought the central RehabAI experience together: an exercise library, visual setup instructions, camera-position guidance, adjustable exercise time, and instructions that can be read aloud.

During supported exercises, users can view motion tracking, receive real-time feedback, and see a score that reflects their exercise performance. The Progress area helps users review exercise scores and repetition trends over time.

Why this release matters

Watching an exercise video can show what a movement should look like, but it cannot respond to the person exercising. RehabAI’s first release begins addressing that gap with feedback during the movement and a measurable record afterward.

What comes next

The first release is a foundation. Future updates will focus on reliability, clearer guidance, a smoother exercise experience, and broader functionality informed by real use.

RehabAI supports at-home exercise and does not replace a physician, physical therapist, diagnosis, treatment plan, or individualized medical advice.
