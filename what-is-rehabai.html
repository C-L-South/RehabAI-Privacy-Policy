What is RehabAI? Core functionality explained

By RehabAI · September 29, 2026

RehabAI is a home rehabilitation exercise app designed to help patients follow an exercise routine with clear guidance, live movement feedback, and scoring.

What problem is RehabAI designed to address?

Patients often complete prescribed exercises at home, away from a healthcare professional. That can make it difficult to know whether an exercise is being performed as intended. RehabAI is designed to make the home exercise experience clearer and more engaging between appointments.

Exercise setup and guidance

The exercise setup screen identifies the selected movement, shows an example, and gives positioning guidance before the session begins. Users can set the exercise duration and switch between available exercises.

An instructions read-aloud option is included in the current interface preview to make guidance easier to follow without continually looking at the screen.

Live movement feedback and scoring

During an exercise, the preview uses connected body-position markers to show how movement is being interpreted. A visible score and progress bar give the user immediate feedback during the activity.

This feedback is intended to help users stay engaged and better understand their exercise performance. RehabAI does not replace a physician, physical therapist, diagnosis, or individualized medical advice.

Progress and personalization

The app navigation includes areas for exercises, progress, and settings. These sections are designed to help users move through their routine, review activity, and adjust their experience as RehabAI continues to develop.

Who is RehabAI for?

RehabAI is being designed for patients who have been given exercises to complete at home and want clearer support while practicing them. Patients should follow the instructions of their qualified healthcare professional and ask that professional whether an exercise is appropriate for them.

Is RehabAI available now?

RehabAI is currently presented as coming soon. The website’s images demonstrate the interface and planned experience; they do not announce a public release or make a claim of clinical effectiveness.
